Dia descends from Arc, and it shows — it is the best-*designed* thing in this category by a comfortable margin. The core interaction is an assistant with awareness of **every tab you have open**, which turns the browser into a reading and synthesis surface rather than an execution surface. "Compare these six pricing pages," "what do these four founder interviews agree on," "draft a summary from what's in front of me."

That makes it a different tool from [[tools/comet|Comet]] and [[tools/aside|Aside]] despite sitting in the same category. Dia is comparatively restrained about autonomously operating your logged-in accounts. For a VC that maps cleanly onto a real split in the work: **Dia for the diligence read**, something more agentic for the export-and-file jobs. It's also the lowest-risk entry point for anyone whose firm is nervous about the prompt-injection exposure that comes with letting an agent act inside authenticated sessions — less autonomy is less blast radius.

The Browser Company was acquired by Atlassian, which is worth factoring into any bet on longevity: this category has already seen one major standalone browser (ChatGPT Atlas) launch and sunset inside nine months.
